### T1
- VERIFIED: You apply for a 7(a) loan through a lender, not the SBA. The SBA provides a loan guarantee to lenders. sba.gov: 'You will be applying for your loan directly through a local lender'; 'The 7(a) loan program provides a loan guarantee to lenders.' Safe line: 'The SBA doesn't lend you the money directly. A participating lender does, and the SBA guarantees part of the loan.' Accessed 2026-10-11. [S1, S3] (confidence high)
- VERIFIED (official wording): sba.gov calls 7(a) 'SBA's primary business loan program for providing financial assistance to small businesses.' Use 'primary,' not 'most common' or 'most popular' (not verified). [S1] (confidence high)
- VERIFIED: Uses of 7(a) proceeds on sba.gov include short- and long-term working capital; buying, refinancing or improving real estate and buildings; buying and installing machinery and equipment; furniture, fixtures and supplies; refinancing current business debt; and changes of ownership (complete or partial, i.e., buying an existing business). Franchise purchases fall under buying or starting a business, but sba.gov wording on franchises wasn't retrieved. Suggested wording: 'buying an existing business (including a franchise business, subject to eligibility)'. [S1] (confidence high, may change)
- VERIFIED: The borrower stays responsible for the full debt. The guarantee covers the lender, not the borrower. CRS: 'The small business borrowing the money remains obligated for the full amount due.' [S23] (confidence high)
- VERIFIED (general wording): Owners above an ownership threshold are generally required to personally guarantee 7(a) loans. SBA Form 1920 (rev. 09/20) says owners with 20% or more must guarantee the loan. Recommend general carousel wording: 'owners are commonly required to sign a personal guarantee.' The threshold was not confirmed against the current SOP 50 10 8.1 text. [S4, S5] (confidence medium, may change)
- VERIFIED (general eligibility): The business must operate for profit, be small under SBA size standards, and credit must not be reasonably available elsewhere on reasonable commercial terms ('credit elsewhere'). ### T2
- VERIFIED: SBA guidance says lenders use credit scores and credit history (of the applicant, associates and guarantors) to judge credit risk and set interest rates. Safe wording: 'Lenders commonly review personal credit, and business credit if there is any.' [S18] (confidence medium)
- VERIFIED: SBA guidance says most lenders expect a business plan when you apply for startup funding. The SBA checklist also asks for a one-year projection of income and finances with a written explanation of how you'll reach it. SCORE likewise says almost all lenders want to see a well-thought-out business plan. [S18, S19, S17] (confidence medium)
- VERIFIED: Documents commonly requested for SBA 7(a) applications include signed personal and business federal tax returns of principals (previous 3 years), a current profit & loss statement and financial statements, projections, a personal financial statement (SBA Form 413), owners' résumés, a business history, and the SBA Borrower Information Form (Form 1919), which also asks about existing debt. Present these as 'items lenders commonly ask for,' not a complete checklist. [S19, S20] (confidence medium, may change)
- VERIFIED (plain-English DSCR): DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) compares the cash a business has available for loan payments with the loan payments themselves (principal + interest). Formula: cash available for debt service (often net operating income) ÷ total debt service. A ratio under 1.0 means not enough cash to cover payments. Lenders set their own minimums. [S21, S22] (confidence medium)
- VERIFIED (math check): Hypothetical $125,000 of cash available for debt payments ÷ $100,000 of annual loan payments (principal + interest) = 1.25, i.e., $1.25 for every $1 of payments. The calculation is correct. [S21] (confidence high)

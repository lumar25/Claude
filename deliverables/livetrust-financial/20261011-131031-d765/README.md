## Topics (AG-003)

- **Carousel A (Pillar 1): SBA 7(a) loans explained: who lends, who guarantees, and how it works** — KEY MESSAGE: An SBA 7(a) loan is made by a lender (a bank or other approved lender), not by the SBA. The SBA guarantees part of the loan, which lowers the lender's risk. The borrower still owes the full amount to the lender.

HOOK ANGLE: Bust the most common myth right away: "The SBA doesn't actually lend you the money." It's surprising and it teaches something, without hype.

7-SLIDE OUTLINE (one idea per slide):
1. HOOK: "The SBA doesn't lend you the money. Here's how SBA 7(a) loans actually work."
2. WHAT IT IS: SBA 7(a) is a federal small business loan program run by the U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA). Explain both terms: "SBA" and "7(a)" (the section of the Small Business Act it comes from). Add a size or popularity claim (e.g. "most common") only if AG-002 verifies it.
3. WHO DOES WHAT: Three roles. The lender reviews the application, approves or declines, and funds the loan. The SBA guarantees a portion of the loan to the lender. The borrower repays the lender. Explain "guarantee" in plain English: if the borrower defaults, the SBA covers part of the lender's loss.
4. COMMON USES: Working capital (explain: money for day-to-day operations), equipment, real estate, and buying an existing business or a franchise. Use only the uses AG-002 confirms in official SBA material.
5. THE PROCESS, AT A HIGH LEVEL: Get your documents ready → find a lender that offers SBA loans → apply → the lender reviews ("underwriting," explained) → a decision → closing and funding. NO timelines. Add a note that steps vary by lender.
6. MYTH #2: "The SBA guarantee protects me, the borrower." Reality: the guarantee protects the lender. The borrower is responsible for repaying the full loan, and owners are often asked for a personal guarantee (wording depends on what AG-002 verifies).
7. CTA: "Have questions about financing your business? Send us a DM." Optional teaser: "Next up: what lenders commonly look at." No URL or handle.

3. CASH INJECTION / EQUITY: Explain it as the money you put into the deal yourself ("skin in the game"). Lenders commonly expect some. The amount varies by lender, program and deal type. Give no percentage unless AG-002 verifies an official SBA requirement with a date. Otherwise say "varies."

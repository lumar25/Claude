## Slide 2 (content)
**What is an SBA 7(a) loan?**

The Small Business Administration (SBA) is a federal agency. It calls 7(a) its primary business loan program. The name comes from the section of federal law that created it.

Most 7(a) loans: up to $5 million*

*Per sba.gov; as of Oct. 11, 2026, subject to change.

## Slide 3 (content)
**Three roles: lender, SBA, borrower**

Lender: reviews your application, makes the decision and funds the loan.

SBA: guarantees part of the loan. If the borrower defaults, the SBA covers part of the lender's loss.

Borrower: repays the lender.

## Slide 4 (content)
**Common uses of 7(a) loans**

• Working capital (money for day-to-day operations)
• Equipment
• Real estate
• Refinancing business debt
• Buying an existing business, including a franchise business (subject to eligibility)

## Slide 5 (content)
**The process, at a high level**

1. Prepare your documents
2. Find a lender that offers SBA loans
3. Apply
4. Underwriting (the lender's review of your finances and risk)
5. Decision
6. Closing and funding

Steps vary by lender. The SBA may also review some loans.

## Slide 6 (content)
**Myth #2: The guarantee protects you**

MYTH: The SBA guarantee protects the borrower.

REALITY: It protects the lender. You still owe the full amount. Owners are commonly required to sign a personal guarantee, a promise to repay personally if the business can't.

## Caption

Many people think getting an SBA loan means applying to the government. You actually apply through a lender, and the SBA guarantees part of the loan to that lender.

A few more points worth knowing:

• Basic eligibility generally includes operating for profit, being "small" under SBA size standards, and passing the "credit elsewhere" test. That test means credit isn't reasonably available elsewhere on reasonable commercial terms.

• Who makes the call? Some lenders make the final credit decision under authority the SBA delegates to them. For other loans, the SBA also reviews the loan. Either way, your application goes through the lender.

• The rules get updated. The SBA's lending rulebook (SOP 50 10 8.1) applies to loans that receive SBA loan numbers on or after October 1, 2026. Always check the current rules before relying on any figure.

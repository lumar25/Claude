What is an SBA 7(a) loan?

The Small Business Administration (SBA) is a federal agency. It calls 7(a) its primary business loan program. The name comes from the section of federal law that created it. Most 7(a) loans: up to $5 million*

Three roles: lender, SBA, borrower

  • Lender: reviews your application, makes the decision and funds the loan.
  • SBA: guarantees part of the loan. If the borrower defaults, the SBA covers part of the lender's loss.
  • Borrower: repays the lender.

Common uses of 7(a) loans

  • Working capital (money for day-to-day operations)
  • Equipment
  • Real estate
  • Refinancing business debt
  • Buying an existing business, including a franchise business (subject to eligibility)

The process, at a high level

  • 1. Prepare your documents
  • 2. Find a lender that offers SBA loans
  • 3. Apply
  • 4. Underwriting (the lender's review of your finances and risk)
  • 5. Decision
  • 6. Closing and funding
  • Steps vary by lender. The SBA may also review some loans.

Myth #2: The guarantee protects you

MYTH: The SBA guarantee protects the borrower. REALITY: It protects the lender. You still owe the full amount. Owners are commonly required to sign a personal guarantee, a promise to repay personally if the business can't.

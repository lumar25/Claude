5 things lenders commonly look at

Thinking about a business loan? Here's what lenders commonly review before making a decision, explained in plain English. Every lender is different. This is a general overview.

1. Credit history

Your credit history is your record of how you've handled past debts. Lenders commonly review your personal credit, plus business credit if the business has any. Expectations vary by lender.

2. Cash injection: your skin in the game

A cash injection (also called equity) is money you put into the deal yourself. Lenders commonly expect some. How much varies by lender, program and type of deal.

3. Cash flow and DSCR

Cash flow: money coming in vs. going out. DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) compares the cash available for loan payments with the payments themselves. Example: $140K ÷ $100K = 1.40

4. Documents

Lenders commonly ask for items such as:

  • Personal and business tax returns
  • A profit & loss statement (income and expenses over a period)
  • A personal financial statement
  • Owner résumés
  • The exact list varies.

5. Business plan and projections

Projections are your estimates of future revenue, expenses and cash flow, plus the assumptions behind them. Most lenders expect a business plan for startup funding, when there's little or no track record.

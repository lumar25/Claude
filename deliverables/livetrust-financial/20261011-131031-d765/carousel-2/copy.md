# Carousel 2: Getting loan ready: 5 things lenders commonly look at

Client: Livetrust Financial  |  Status: PENDING YOUR APPROVAL

## Slide 1 (hook)
**5 things lenders commonly look at**

Thinking about a business loan? Here's what lenders commonly review before making a decision, explained in plain English.

Every lender is different. This is a general overview.

## Slide 2 (content)
**1. Credit history**

Your credit history is your record of how you've handled past debts. Lenders commonly review your personal credit, plus business credit if the business has any.

Expectations vary by lender.

## Slide 3 (content)
**2. Cash injection: your skin in the game**

A cash injection (also called equity) is money you put into the deal yourself. Lenders commonly expect some.

How much varies by lender, program and type of deal.

## Slide 4 (content)
**3. Cash flow and DSCR**

Cash flow: money coming in vs. going out.

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) compares the cash available for loan payments with the payments themselves.

Example: $140K ÷ $100K = 1.40

*Hypothetical illustration, not a lender requirement.

## Slide 5 (content)
**4. Documents**

Lenders commonly ask for items such as:
• Personal and business tax returns
• A profit & loss statement (income and expenses over a period)
• A personal financial statement
• Owner résumés

The exact list varies.

## Slide 6 (content)
**5. Business plan and projections**

Projections are your estimates of future revenue, expenses and cash flow, plus the assumptions behind them.

Most lenders expect a business plan for startup funding, when there's little or no track record.

## Caption

Being "loan ready" isn't about hitting one magic number. It's about understanding what lenders commonly review and getting organized before you apply.

A few extra notes:

• Credit matters beyond the yes-or-no decision. Lenders also use credit scores and credit history to help set interest rates.

• About DSCR: a ratio below 1.0 means the business isn't generating enough cash to cover its loan payments. Lenders set their own minimums, and program rules can also apply, so there's no single universal target.

• For SBA 7(a) applications, owners commonly provide a personal financial statement, and the SBA's Borrower Information Form asks about existing debt. Keeping a current list of what the business owes makes this step easier.

• Write down the assumptions behind your projections, such as where your revenue estimate comes from. That makes them easier to explain.

## Facts per topic

### T1
- Slide 2 framing is supported: SBA 7(a) loans are made by lenders (SBA 'participating lenders'), and the SBA provides a guarantee to those lenders. Suggested one-liner: 'SBA loans are made by banks and other approved lenders, and the SBA guarantees part of the loan. The lender still reviews and decides on each application.' This avoids implying SBA endorsement. [S2, S1] (confidence high)
- SBA lists short- and long-term working capital as an allowed use of 7(a) proceeds. [S1, S3] (confidence high, may change)
- SBA lists changes of ownership (complete or partial) as an allowed use of 7(a) proceeds. This supports 'buying an existing business' as an example. Partial changes of ownership have been allowed since May 2023. [S1, S3] (confidence high, may change)
- Inventory is a listed loan purpose on SBA Form 1919, the 7(a) borrower form. Equipment, furniture, fixtures and supplies are also listed uses. [S1] (confidence high, may change)
- Use-of-funds breakdown is supported: SBA Form 1919 asks the applicant to split the loan request into 'Purpose of the loan' amounts that add up to the total request. SBA's business plan guidance tells borrowers to give 'a detailed description of how you'll use your funds.' So 'lenders commonly ask for a clear breakdown of how the money will be used' is accurate. 'Use of proceeds' is the formal term. [S6] (confidence high)
- SBA and the IRS both recommend keeping separate business and personal bank accounts. SBA says mixing the two leads to 'an accounting nightmare' and that separation supports clean, accurate bookkeeping. These sources present it as general best practice, not as a formal SBA loan requirement. [S7, S8] (confidence high)
- A reputable non-government source says lenders want to see consistent business deposits and expenses in a dedicated account, and that mixed spending makes real business cash flow harder to show. This supports 'often' or 'can make it harder' wording. [S11] (confidence medium)
- Underwriting definition is supported: SBA material describes underwriting as the lender's process for reviewing a loan, including financial analysis of the business and its ability to repay from cash flow. 'The lender's review of your application, including whether the business can repay' is an accurate plain-English definition. [S9] (confidence medium)
- SBA's business plan guidance tells established businesses to provide income statements, balance sheets and cash flow statements, and tells new businesses to provide projections. This supports the idea that lenders compare financial records, so they should be current and consistent. [S6] (confidence medium)
- UNVERIFIED: Franchise fees and closing costs as allowed 7(a) uses: no official SBA source was retrieved that lists 'franchise fees' or 'closing costs' by name. Third-party guides say franchise fees can sometimes be included, but also that many lenders require them to be paid before closing. On slide 4, frame these as 'costs to consider in your budget,' not 'costs the loan can cover.'
- UNVERIFIED: That 'numbers not matching across tax returns, financial statements and bank statements' is a commonly cited lender problem: no official source states this directly. Safer wording: 'Lenders review your tax returns, financial statements and bank statements together, so it helps when they tell a consistent story.'
- UNVERIFIED: That 'asking for too much without justification' is a common reason for problems: not directly sourced. Safer wording: 'Asking for more than you can explain can raise questions. Every dollar should tie back to your use of funds.'
- UNVERIFIED: That owners 'often begin looking for financing after signing' agreements (frequency claim, Mistake #1): no source found. Safer wording: 'Some owners start looking for financing only after they've signed a purchase agreement, franchise agreement or lease. That can leave less room to prepare.'
- UNVERIFIED: That out-of-date bookkeeping is a 'commonly cited' lender problem: only general best-practice support was found (S7, S8, S6). Safer wording: 'Keeping your books current makes it easier to provide the financial statements lenders typically review.'

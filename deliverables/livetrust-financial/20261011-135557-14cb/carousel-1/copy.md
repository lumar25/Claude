## Slide 1 (hook)
**3 common SBA loan application mistakes**

All three are things you can control: timing, amount and organization.

## Slide 2 (content)
**First, a quick reminder**

SBA loans are made by banks and other approved lenders, and the SBA guarantees part of the loan.
The lender still reviews and decides on each application, so preparation matters.

## Slide 3 (content)
**Mistake #1: Starting too late**

Mistake: Looking for financing only after signing a purchase agreement or lease.
Why it hurts: Less room to compare lenders or prepare for underwriting (the lender's review).
Instead: Start the conversation early.

## Slide 4 (content)
**Mistake #2: Asking for the wrong amount**

Mistake: Sizing the loan to the purchase price only.
Why it hurts: You can miss working capital (day-to-day money) or inventory. Unexplained extras can raise questions.
Instead: List what every dollar pays for (use of funds).

## Slide 5 (content)
**Mistake #3: Messy or mixed finances**

Mistake: Mixed personal and business spending, or books that aren't current.
Why it hurts: Mixing can hide real cash flow. Lenders typically review your financial records together.
Instead: Separate accounts, current books, consistent numbers.

## Caption

Applying for an SBA loan? A lot of friction comes from things you control: when you start, how much you ask for, and how organized your finances are.

1) Starting too late. Some owners look for financing only after signing a purchase agreement, franchise agreement or lease. That leaves less room to compare lenders and prepare for underwriting: the lender's review of your application, including whether the business can repay.

2) Asking for the wrong amount. Look beyond the price tag. Working capital (money for day-to-day operations), inventory, franchise-related costs and closing costs (costs paid to finalize the loan or purchase) are worth considering in your budget. A clear use-of-funds breakdown shows exactly what the money will pay for.

3) Messy or mixed finances. Keep separate business and personal accounts, keep your books current, and make sure your numbers tell a consistent story.

Avoiding these mistakes doesn't guarantee approval, and every lender is different. But it can make the process smoother.
